# Bathhouse Row facades and Fordyce interior (panorama reference)

Scope: the Row as seen from the west side of Central Avenue, looking east, plus the Fordyce interior. This builds on `research/visual-style-guide.md` ("Architecture by building", "Skyline recipe and row order") and `research/bathhouse-row-and-springs.md`; it doesn't repeat their history. Conflicts with those files are marked **conflict**.

**How the numbers were measured.** Each building's numbers come from the scanned drawing sheets. The scans are about 401 dpi, so the 3/16" elevations work out to about 75 px per foot and the 1"=50' site plan to about 8 px per foot. Both rates match the scale bars on the sheets. The street silhouette has no scale bar of its own. I assumed it uses the plan scale; its total length (about 1,166 ft) matches the plan, and its widths and heights agree with the elevations within about 5%. Heights are measured above the grade at each front, ±1–2 ft. These are measured values (HABS 1984), not published dimensions.

This resolves the "[UNVERIFIED] confirm against an NPS map" note in the existing research files. NRHP: "The northernmost bathhouse on the row is the Superior."

**Gaps between buildings** (plan silhouette, measured): Superior–Hale ~2 ft · Hale–Maurice ~57 ft (garden and the 1903 Maurice Spring fountain and retaining wall, NRHP) · Maurice–Fordyce ~41 ft (Main Entrance and eagles) · Fordyce–Quapaw ~31 ft · Quapaw–Ozark ~31 ft · Ozark–Buckstaff ~19 ft · Buckstaff–Lamar ~10 ft · Lamar–Admin ~11 ft. **Total run** from Superior's north wall to the Admin Building's south wall is **~1,165 ft**. Grade at the fronts falls about 5–6 ft from north to south on the silhouette [measured, approximate].

**Relative heights (main mass)**, from the silhouette and elevations: Fordyce (~45–49) ≈ Maurice centre (~48) ≈ Buckstaff centre (~45) > Hale ridge (~37–38) ≈ Admin ridge (~38) ≈ Quapaw main ridge (~35) > Lamar (~31–34) ≈ Superior (~30) > Ozark parapet (~26, towers ~38–41). **The highest point is the Quapaw lantern, ~58–60 ft.** The three-storey Fordyce, Maurice and Buckstaff form the tall middle-north and south masses. The NRHP calls all the buildings "of similar height, scale, and proportions".

**Setback and landscaping.** NRHP: "All of the buildings are set back the same distance from the sidewalk, and have garden areas and green spaces in front"; the gaps between buildings are softened by "smaller hedges and bushes". On the HABS site plan, the main walls stand roughly 20 ft behind the sidewalk at the north end (Superior, Hale) and about 30–45 ft at the south end (Buckstaff, Lamar), because Central Ave curves [measured, ±10 ft; the 1985 NRHP calls the setback uniform]. The 1984 photos show clipped hedges along the walk, lawns, and a straight path to each door [observed].

## Behind and around the Row

- **Grand Promenade.** A brick walk running north–south on the hillside directly behind (east of) the bathhouses, from Reserve Ave to Fountain St; begun in the 1930s and finished in the early 1960s (NRHP). It is shown between two tree belts on the HABS plan. It sits up the slope above retaining walls, so from Central Ave it is mostly hidden by the buildings and shows through the gaps, best at the Main Entrance, where the **Stevens Balustrade** (limestone ashlar and concrete, with a baroque double staircase and a vaulted hemicycle fountain niche) climbs to it (NRHP). A bandstand stood on the balustrade until 1958.
- **Hot Springs Mountain.** A wooded slope rises directly behind the roofs; the 1984 Hale photo shows treetops above its ridge [observed].
- **Mountain Tower (1983).** 216 ft tall; upper deck at 1,256 ft above sea level ([NPS](https://www.nps.gov/places/hot-springs-mountain-tower.htm)); at 34.5172 N, 93.0461 W ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Springs_Mountain_Tower)). [inferred] Using the estimated Fordyce position (34.5141, −93.0535): the tower is about 2,500 ft away on a bearing of about 63° (ENE), so from across the street it stands **behind the north half of the Row (over Maurice, Hale or Superior)**, not centred over the Fordyce. Its top is about 650–700 ft above street level (street ~600 ft, not sourced), an elevation angle of only about 15°. The rooftops of 45-ft buildings seen from about 100 ft away subtend about 24°, so the tower is **hidden or barely visible from the near sidewalk** and shows above the roofs only from farther west or north, or from higher up. No source confirms either way; a panorama can show it small, high on the ridge at the left.
- **Arlington Hotel.** At the north end, at the "Y" of Central Ave and Fountain St, at "the terminus of the town's most important vista" ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arlington_Hotel_(Hot_Springs_National_Park))). From a viewer facing east it is off to the left, past Superior. Its height and storey count were not found; "much taller than the Row" is [inferred].
- **Magnolia Promenade.** Laid out by Army engineer Robert Stevens: "double rows of magnolias during the 1890s, but now a single row separates the sidewalk and the street" (NRHP). On the HABS plan the trees stand in one line along the curb, about **25 ft apart** [measured]. In the 1984 photos the magnolia canopies overhang the walk and frame every facade [observed].
- **Street lamps.** In 1916 Jens Jensen had "lights … placed along the street promenade" (NRHP). The 1984 photos show **dark green cast-iron posts with a cluster of 5 white globe lamps** (one on top, four on arms) along the Central Ave walk and at the Main Entrance; HABS AR-28-I-6 records a "typical light standard" [observed]. Whether these posts date from 1916 is unknown.
- **Across Central Ave.** The west side is the commercial Central Avenue Historic District. On the HABS plan it is about 70–75 ft from the east curb to the west building line [measured, approximate], which sets the viewer's distance for the panorama.

## Conflicts and corrections to the existing research files

1. **Admin Building orientation.** It faces **south onto Reserve Ave**: HABS calls it "the only structure not facing Central Avenue" and its photos are of the "south front". `visual-style-guide.md` says it "faces the street"; that is true only if the street is Reserve. From Central Ave you see its plain two-storey west side.
2. **Admin Building date.** 1936 (NRHP; HABS AR-28-I) vs "Built in 1938" (HABS AR-28 data page). Prefer 1936.
3. **Hale Mann & Stern remodel.** 1919 (NRHP) vs 1914 (Wikipedia, as cited in the existing research). Storeys: NRHP "two and one half"; the HABS elevation shows 2 storeys under the hip roof.
4. **Hale bell gable.** It sits on the one-storey porch, not on the main roofline; the `visual-style-guide.md` recipe ("centered double-curve parapet") should be drawn low.
5. **Fordyce roof.** Not one red hip roof but two low hipped end pavilions with a flat centre, plus a set-back hipped skylight/penthouse. The curvilinear parapets are on the side (end) walls.
6. **Buckstaff.** NRHP says "engaged columns"; HABS calls them the "large Doric columns of the colonnade" (with entasis). Brick: NRHP "cream" vs the 1984 photo's tan-brown.
7. **HABS caption dates** ("circa 1920" for Superior, Ozark, Buckstaff and Lamar) are approximate; prefer the NRHP dates (1916, 1922, 1912, 1923).
8. **Fordyce brick colour.** NRHP "cream" vs the photo's golden buff with darker lozenge outlines. Lighting and film may shift colour; treat photo colours as [observed 1984], not as specification.
